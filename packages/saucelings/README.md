# Saucelings

Saucelings is a Tamagotchi-style pet package for Marinara Engine that lives in **Home → Saucelings** and on the Home widget grid.

Pick a character to look after a sauceling. They bring an egg that matches their personality, then feed, play with, and cuddle it on their own schedule according to a care profile distilled from their character card. You open Home, hear from them what they did, and cover the gaps they missed.

The caretaker speaks in their own words: they tell you what happened, and talk to the sauceling while they look after it. Their care profile reads every part of the card that shows how they talk (description, personality, backstory, scenario, system prompt and other instructions, example dialogue and first message) and gives them a speech style and a few lines of their own for everyday care. Lines written by your text connection also see those card fields. That voice is made only when you ask: when a character without one becomes the caretaker (bringing an egg, a switch under **About**, or **Bring back** from the Menagerie), Saucelings offers to read their card once with your text connection. **Not now** keeps lines guessed from the card, and the offer comes back on the next switch. An edited card keeps its voice; **About → Refresh voice** reads the card again. A caretaker you switch to under **About** says hello to the sauceling in their own words, so the box shows their name at once. Lines you ask for with a click (Check in, a new caretaker's hello, a welcome back, a farewell) don't count against the daily limit in **Settings**; past it, other events keep their template line.

How the two of you care for a pasta sauceling shapes its sauce temperament (`Marinara` at hatch, shifting into `Zesto`, `Carbonuzzle`, `Rabbratty`, `Seppy`, `Alfredoze`, or `Truffoddity`). A sauceling that isn't pasta (a Mossaboo, say, or a found one) has a temperament of its own instead: at hatch your text connection writes one that takes after whoever found its egg, and after two weeks with another caretaker it moves a step toward theirs, up to three steps two weeks apart. The caretaker's lines follow the temperament. Every sauceling keeps the name of whoever found its egg; **About** and its Menagerie tile show it. At hatch your text connection also gives it a name that plays on what it is, in one call that doesn't count against the daily limit; without a connection it keeps its kind's name until you name it. Elders can retire to the **Menagerie** with a farewell scene, and any retired elder can return to the nook locked at whichever post-egg stage you choose. **Swap** sends the current sauceling to the Menagerie and brings a new egg; **Release** lets a Menagerie sauceling go for good. The Menagerie shows its saucelings as tiles grouped by stage, elders first; open one to read its farewell, bring it back or release it. **Looked after by** picks who it comes back to: the caretaker it had, or another of your characters. A sauceling that never lived in the nook (an egg found while chatting) waits for you to pick one.

An egg hatches into one of fifteen species. Rigamuffin, Fluttino, Lentino, Shellbini, Tortellump, Mossaboo, Puddlepip, Snufflump and Burrowl can come from any egg, and a caretaker's card leans towards some of them. Wickwisp, Cauldrop and Webbit come only from eggs brought in October (UTC); one you already have stays. Prismite, Thundrake and Clockrit are special: each is unlocked once, by raising 3 saucelings to elder, by 100 of your care actions, or by your care on 30 different days, and stays unlocked. The Adopt and Swap panels show how far you are under **Special eggs**. Eggs found while chatting are always one of the first nine kinds, or a creature the caretaker invents.

The **Nook** is built around a stage: a 16:9 pixel-art background with your sauceling standing on it. Each species has its own built-in background, and every egg shares one nest, so the species stays a surprise until it hatches. The egg sits in the nest; drag it, tap where it should go or use the arrow keys, and it stays there on that background. The sauceling walks (in side view), turns, hums, hops and naps by itself, and comes over when you tap the stage; it and the caretaker hold still when the Engine's reduced effects or your system's reduced motion is on. You can replace the background with a picture you upload (resized in your browser to at most 1280×720) or one generated through the same image connection the Album uses. The prompt box starts with the species' own background (the nest for an egg, a room in its own colours for an invented sauceling), and **Suggest one** asks the writing connection for a place that suits this sauceling, its look, traits and caretaker, to edit before you generate; an egg's kind stays a surprise. Uploaded and generated backgrounds stay in a library shared by every sauceling, up to 24: **Background** picks one again or deletes one. When the caretaker has sprites in the Engine (expression sprites, or full-body ones when they have only those), they stand at the right of the stage, drawn as the Engine's chat draws them and settling gently in place, and the sauceling keeps to the rest of it; they look happy for a moment after good news, after you fill a gap and as you care for the sauceling, worried while a meter is low, and calm otherwise. Their message box is joined under the stage; a caretaker without sprites has their avatar there. On a wide panel Check in and Ask sit in the box and the meters and care buttons beside the stage; on a narrow one Check in and Ask follow the care buttons. A new line types out (all at once with reduced motion), and the sauceling turns to the caretaker and hops. When their latest line is about feeding, playing with or cuddling the sauceling, the stage plays that care once each time the page is opened. Before any care plays, yours or theirs, the sauceling hurries over to a caretaker standing on the stage, as far as its walking space goes, and turns to face them; an egg stays in its nest. With image generation on, **About → Paint <name> for the Nook** paints the caretaker as a pixel figure in the saucelings' style, in one image request from their avatar, one of their sprites and their card's appearance (its image appearance when that is turned on). If the drawings can't be read, it asks once more without the reference pictures; with **Simpler caretaker figures** on in **Settings**, it then asks for just three drawings in one row (calm, happy and worried), which weaker image models manage more often. Your browser cuts the eight drawings into a 768×96 strip: five facing you (calm, happy, worried, talking and waving) and three side views facing the sauceling (feeding, playing and tending). Drawings that touch are cut apart, and four over four are read as well; when only the top row can be read, the figure keeps its first three poses until a Repaint. The figure stands on the stage in place of their sprites: it waves through a hello, talks while any other line comes in, turns to the sauceling for two seconds when care plays, theirs or yours (the bowl for Feed, the toy for Play, reaching down for Cuddle, Rest and Warm), and otherwise looks as their sprites would; it breathes as the saucelings do, sinking one pixel at the chest, and blinks now and then when your browser can find its eyes in the drawing (a face behind a mask or hair may keep them open). The old figure or sprite stays until the new one is saved, and **Undo <name>'s repaint** goes back to it (again to bring the repaint back). **Flip <name>** (in About, for a caretaker painted or not) shows them facing the other way in the Nook, with no image request. A figure painted before 0.6.0 keeps its three looks (calm, happy and worried). The large Home widget is the same stage filling the whole card, with the caretaker on it, thin bars for the meters along its top edge (hover one for its name) and round Feed and Play buttons beside them; on a narrow Home the stage keeps 16:9 and the bars and buttons sit under it. The sauceling walks there only while Home is open. The package itself never contacts an image service.

Optional paintings fill the **Album** tab. Each one is drawn by an image-generation connection you pick in **Settings**, through Marinara Engine's own avatar generator with its style profile set to Off and Saucelings' own prompt in place of the Engine's avatar prompt, so the paintings keep the sprites' pixel-art style. Each request sends the sauceling's sprite as a reference picture, so the image model paints the same creature; models that take no reference pictures ignore it. **Add instructions** under the Paint button takes up to 200 characters about what the scene should show; they replace the plain background, may bring your caretaker into the picture, and are kept as the painting's caption. The Album keeps every painting until you delete it, including paintings of released saucelings. The package never contacts an image service itself.

**Egg hunting** (in **Settings**, on by default): each time a character replies in a chat, they may find an egg, as long as the nook has a sauceling. The chance is 1 in 40, halved for each egg they found that is still unhatched, down to 1 in 320, and at most one find a day. Your text connection invents the creature from their card (a kind, a look, colours, traits, a quirk, and a picture description for each stage); it is never pasta. The egg waits in the Menagerie, and the caretaker tells you in the nook, naming who found it. An adult can also evolve once, at 1 in 60 a reply, into a creature that fits its caretaker; it keeps its name, sauce, meters and journal. Without a text connection a find is an ordinary egg and nothing evolves.

Egg hunting uses the Engine's prompt-context permission only to notice that a reply is being written: the package adds nothing to the prompt and reads no chat text. The Engine also counts Peek Prompt, regenerate, swipe, impersonate and failed generations as replies. In a group chat with no reply target every character counts. Multiplayer rooms never roll.

An invented sauceling has no sprite of its own. With image generation on, its whole life is painted in one picture when you agree: the first time the Nook or the Album shows a stage with no picture, a dialog asks whether to paint it (once for each stage), and a Paint button stays for later. The picture is a grid with the egg and, for every stage, the creature happy, content and grumpy from the front and once from the side. An evolved sauceling's picture holds its adult and elder stages and starts from its old sprite. A found egg is painted once it comes to the nook. Your browser reads the grid, cuts each drawing out of its background, gives the whole creature one palette and makes the squash and stretch frames, so the forms move like the built-in species and walk in side view. Each form is stored as a 312×104 strip. When the picture cannot be read, the stage on show is painted alone, from the form before it, and walks by turning its front view. Only one open page paints a sauceling at a time. When a picture comes out wrong, **About → Repaint** paints every stage again in one picture; the old forms stay until the new ones are saved, and **About → Undo repaint** goes back to them (again to bring the repaint back; a sheet repaint keeps the whole set). When a sauceling walks the wrong way round, **About → Flip <name>** turns it for every stage, with no image request. Until a form is painted, a found sauceling shows a silhouette and an evolved one keeps its old sprite.
