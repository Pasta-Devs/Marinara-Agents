## Faces and worn outfits as image references (1.27.1)

Scene, banner and Bond art now sends two references for each character in the picture: who they are, then what they wear. Who they are is their Engine character card's avatar, your persona's avatar for the protagonist, or, without either, the unit's saved portrait. What they wear is the image of the outfit they have on, sent only when the picture paints them in it, so the image never contradicts the prompt. Gacha banners and Bond backgrounds now paint a unit in the outfit it has on while **Outfits** is on, as chat photos already did unless the message says what they wear; a unit with nothing on keeps its whole look. Story scenes, key images and story event art paint the outfit, and send it, only when **The story uses worn outfits** is on (it is off by default) and the scene does not dress them itself; otherwise they paint the default clothes and send the face alone. Before, a character card's unit sent only its card avatar, so its outfit never reached the painter, and a unit without a card sent its outfit in place of its face, even in a picture that painted its default clothes.

Every face goes before any outfit, and at most four images are sent, which is what the Engine's image route keeps. A provider may keep fewer (for example xAI and NanoGPT three; Stability, Together, Atlas Cloud, Automatic1111 and OpenRouter's image models other than GPT Image only the first; NovelAI models other than V4.5 none), so faces are what survive. When the limit leaves out an outfit or a face, the server log says how many. A new portrait, an enemy portrait or a new outfit is painted from the character's face alone, so the outfit they have on now no longer leaks into a new look. Expression portraits still edit the image the story shows. Art already painted is unchanged.

## Hard and Very Hard for finished story events (1.27.0)

A story event is still played on Normal first. Once all its scenes and fights are played, its map opens **Hard**, and once every fight is won on Hard, **Very Hard**, with the same pills, locks and **Recommended BP** as a main chapter. Hard and Very Hard list only the event's fights. They are the fights designed when you played Normal, so no new fight is designed and no model is asked for one, calibrated like the main chapter after the one the event followed: 1.5 and 2 times Normal's power. Each costs 5 Vigor to start, and its first win on each difficulty pays what a main chapter's Hard or Very Hard fight pays: 750 Funds and 150 Aether, or 1,000 and 200, with 300 rank XP and the Hard clear achievements and pass tasks. A fight won on its difficulty does not load or start again, so it pays once. Each difficulty keeps its own progress, and a save from before 1.27.0 opens Hard straight away for an event it finished. The event's own rewards are still Normal's.

They can be played while the event runs and Seasonal events is on. Once the event ends or Seasonal events is turned off, their fights close as Normal's new progress does, and what you won stays. Hard never holds the main story.

In **Chapters**, a finished event shows a cleared chapter's N, H and V marks, and **Enter event** while its Hard fights are open. **Events → Story events** counts its Hard and Very Hard wins. A story event's Normal nodes no longer list a chapter's Funds, Aether and rank, which an event never paid: they show their Vigor cost, and the event's rewards stay on its card. Opening a played event's map no longer waits for a scene being written elsewhere in the world.

## Played story events in Chapters, and fights that say why they can't start (1.26.3)

A story event you have played now sits in **Chapters** right after the chapter it followed, the way a finished Character Story does. Events and Character Stories that followed the same chapter appear in the order you opened them, which is the order the story remembers them in; a Character Story part from before 1.8.12, which has no opening time, comes first. Before, several Character Stories after one chapter were sorted by name. **Events → Story events** also offers **Revisit played scenes** for a finished event instead of **Continue event**. The list below the next chapter now keeps only an event that can start but is not offered in **What happens next**, and an open event while a Character Story holds the main story.

Since 1.18.0 the event's map was meant to hide the Normal, Hard and Very Hard pills, but they stayed visible, so once the event was finished, Hard unlocked and listed its fights with **Start**. Those fights were already cleared on Normal, so the server refused to start them, and **Start battle** on their briefing did nothing. The event's map now draws no difficulty pills, and a cleared event fight is turned away before its briefing, with the reason on screen.

Any fight whose start is refused, in a chapter, Character Story, story event, Materials or the Tower, now shows why on the fight screen, with **Retry**, which loads the fight again, and **Back**. Before, only a lack of Vigor was shown, and any other refusal left an enabled **Start battle** that did nothing.

Two older ways for **Start battle** to do nothing are fixed too. And a chapter, Character Story or story event start still waiting for its answer when you switched worlds left every **Start battle** in the new world doing nothing until a reload; switching worlds now drops the old start, and its late answer, or that of a Materials or Tower start, no longer reaches the new world.

## Fight loading, Formation swaps and memory that keeps going (1.25.2)

A seasonal story event's fights load again in a world where a chapter or Character Story was regenerated. Before, every event fight in such a world stopped at "Couldn't set up the battle.", and Retry repeated the same refusal, because the fight's request was the one event request sent without the story's version. A fight that can't be set up now also shows the reason the server gave, and a story changed in another tab says to reopen it.

Backing out of a fight while it is still loading and entering another one now loads the new fight. Before, the first fight's late reply replaced it, so the fight you left was shown and could be started. A fight request that never answers no longer blocks every later fight either, and Back from a loading Materials or event fight frees those screens at once.

In Formation, a two-unit move (a planned swap, or a Swap effect) seats both units before either one's passive fires, so a revive, Swap, Shuffle or Echo it sets off never sees the seat the mover left as free, and no row ever holds four. A revive returns the fallen unit to the row it fell in when that row has room, otherwise to the other row, or not at all when both are full; a unit revived in another row, or moved by a Shuffle and then defeated, is now drawn where it really is. Fights started before 1.25.2 are scored by the rules they started under.

While the shared overview is behind or set aside, the chapters it does not cover are sent as their summaries. On a small model these could leave too little room for the reply, which then failed. Now older chapter summaries are left out, oldest first, only when the reply would otherwise get less than its 8,192-token minimum. The newest chapter summary, and every Character Story and story event, always stay. A prompt that fits keeps every summary, unchanged.

When an earlier chapter has no summary yet (memory is still catching up, or waits for Retry), the full story history is sent instead. It grew with every chapter, so after a few dozen chapters most models refused it. Now its oldest chapters are left out first, then its oldest Character Stories and story events, only when the reply would otherwise get less than its 8,192-token minimum. The previous chapter and everything after it, the scene so far and your saved decisions always stay, and a history that fits is sent unchanged. Without a known context window the history is still sent whole.

Story memory also no longer stops for good when a chapter, Character Story or event it already summarized reads differently later, for example after an update changes how stored scenes are rendered. Before, it stopped summarizing at that source, so a few chapters later every prompt fell back to the full history. Now the first memory of that source stays canon, the overview built over it stays valid, and later chapters keep being summarized. A replay still never rewrites first play, and a chapter reset on purpose (regenerating an unfinished chapter) still replaces its memory.

## Play while pre-forging and smoother upgrades (1.25.1)

While pre-forge writes a chapter, its map stays playable. ## Story memory recall (1.25.0)

Story memory recalls by meaning as well as by words. When the Engine has an embedding model, each saved memory gets a vector in the background and recall compares meaning alongside words; without one, recall uses words alone. Recall waits at most 1.5 seconds for the embedder, then continues on words.

Recall searches with what was just played or said: the end of the current chapter so far (or of the previous chapter before the first scene), the last fight's outcome, and in a chat the messages you sent since the companion last replied. The chapter plan is only a weak extra cue. Names count for more than common words, except the names of whoever is talking.

Only clearly relevant memories are recalled: up to three chapter summaries and up to three side memories (Character Stories, story events and chats together), within a tenth of the model's context alongside the overview. Conversations a prompt already lists among its newest are not recalled a second time.

A unit's chats stay private and are no longer folded into the shared overview. They are recalled in that unit's own chats, its Character Story planning, scenes and story events it is in, and when a main chapter is planned; never in another unit's chat or in a fight. Another unit's Character Story reaches a chat only once the shared overview covers it, and its decisions are not listed there. An overview saved before 1.25.0 that already holds chats is rebuilt in the background and is not sent to chats or scenes until then.

Every story writer and chat follows a knowledge boundary: a character knows only what they saw, took part in, or learned inside the story from the protagonist or another in-world source. They never know another character's private conversations unless they learned of them that way, and nothing outside the fiction.

## Faster chapter forging and background memory (1.24.9)

A new chapter opens as soon as its plan is written. Its fights are no longer designed before the chapter map appears. Each fight is designed in the background right after the scene before it is written, so it is ready, with its enemy art, when you reach it. With pre-forge on, the write-ahead chain designs it between scenes. With pre-forge off, opening the scene starts it. A fight neither path reached is designed when its battle opens. Pre-forge Cancel, switching to Off, or a pending big choice stops the chain before its next fight or scene.

Automatic continuity summarizes only in the background, so writing a plan, scene, fight or chat no longer waits for it to catch up. Prompts use the overview saved so far, recalled chapter summaries, the previous chapter in full and the current chapter. Canon the overview does not cover yet goes in beside it, in story order: as its saved summary, or as written for a Character Story, story event or chat not summarized yet. Only an earlier chapter with no summary at all falls back to the full history. A summary request already running on the same connection still finishes first; choosing a separate compression connection avoids sharing it. A failed summary waits for **Retry** in Continuity instead of being retried before every generation. The overview is sized once for the smallest of the story, narration and chat models, and Continuity asks for configuration when one of them has no known context size. Each summary gets at least 10k tokens of output room, or the model's maximum, never below 8,192, so long chapters can be summarized by thinking models.

## Story and chat recovery (1.16.1)

## Automatic continuity (1.14.0)

Gacha Forge automatically keeps memories from the world's completed canonical chapters, Character Stories and character conversations. Original records stay saved. Later writing receives the constant overview, up to three relevant detailed chapter summaries, separate relevant character memories, the entire previous chapter, and the current chapter so far, in that order. The full previous chapter does not consume a recall slot. Saved first-play choices remain canonical; replaying an alternative does not replace them.

The overview uses at most 10% of the selected generation model's context window, measured with the Engine's token estimator. If the generation or compression connection's context window is unknown, configure it in **Engine Connections**. Gacha Forge reports the missing configuration instead of guessing a model limit.

Completed Character Stories and story events contribute to the overview; since 1.25.0 a unit's conversations stay private to it (see above). Relevant Character Story, event and conversation details are recalled separately from the three chapters. When space is tight, whole optional recall blocks are removed in relevance order; the complete previous chapter and current played content remain intact. Since 1.25.2, while the overview is behind, older chapter summaries standing in for it may be left out when the reply would not fit otherwise (see above). If required history alone still exceeds the model's context, generation reports the overflow instead of silently shortening it.

**1.8.8 is a lock that shut more than it meant to.** A Character Story holds the main story until it ends, by design: a chapter left half played next to an unfinished Character Story was a state that should not exist. But the three routes that advance the main story (`/chapter-plan`, `/story/start`, `/battle/start`) asked whether a Character Story was open without saying which chapter they were serving, so they also refused chapters already cleared on Normal — while the chapter list drew those as openable, and the client, reading the refusal as "go to the Character Story", bounced the click there. Replaying Hard and Very Hard, or re-reading an old chapter, advances nothing. The lock now takes the chapter: one cleared on Normal is past story and is never held, and the next chapter still waits. There is no chapter in progress to hold: a Character Story is only offered once the chapter before it is cleared.

**1.8.9 is a character drawn a woman and narrated a man.** The portrait is painted from the unit's `appearance`, and a field derived from it (`identitySex`) tells a group scene how to count its subjects. The narrator never received it: the one cast formatter — the planner's, the beat's and the Character Story's — sent name, role, description, personality and clothes, so an ambiguous description let each side guess. That formatter had already solved this for clothes, by sending the same derived line the picture paints from; the sex was simply left out. The sheet and the planner's index line now carry `(she/her)` or `(he/him)` from that one field. The field is binary — the derivation asks male or female only, and a stored `other` reads female, which is how every group picture already counted it, so no drawing changes. And it no longer depends on art: the two prompts that mint a character ask for it in the same call that writes the sheet, and characters created before that are filled in by one call per world the first time the story is told. Its answer is keyed by character number — a bare list is only trusted at its exact length, because one that comes back short would hand a character somebody else's sex — and is saved on each unit; once every character has one, the call is never made again. It cannot stop a plan or a scene, whatever goes wrong inside it, and one rule covers every way a character can be left without a saved answer (the call failing, the model leaving them out, a write losing its revision, an error): the fill waits ten minutes before it is tried again, so the same answer is never paid for on every scene.

**1.8.11 is art paid for twice and a boss that healed off its whole bar.** Every painter asked "is this already painted?" when it was queued, then waited for the image connection's turn; on a slow connection the queue outlived the 20-minute dedupe ceiling, the next request found the lock expired and nothing filed yet, and painted it again. Now every painter — a chapter's backgrounds and enemies, banner art, portraits, key images, Bond backgrounds, the outfit rotation and the Bond outfit — re-reads what is filed with that turn held, and only then paints and files (`paintOnce`). The rotation files each outfit as it lands, merged by unit, instead of all at the end; the banner, key image and portrait writes are checked and retried; a Bond outfit already in the wardrobe with its rung unsettled is settled and handed over. The chat photo is asked once and was left alone. And every heal scales off the healer's max HP, which for a Tower boss is inflated by its boss shape and floor weight: the floor 40 Mender healed 22.5% of that bar per Ultimate. A boss now heals from one of its escorts' bodies (`healAs`, relative to its power), in the fight, the CP and the enemy sheet alike; `healAs` is frozen with the fight so the claim replays what the screen played. The Mender's weight was re-fitted on both Tower benches, 1.54 to 1.15.

**1.8.12 is a Character Story that could only be compressed once.** Compressed chapters can be merged into one entry, as long as they are contiguous; Character Stories arrived later, got their own compression, and were left out of the merge — a comment even decided it the other way. Now the Continuity rows come from one builder (`continuityRows`), in the order the story happened: each Character Story after the chapter it followed, and several after the same chapter in the order they were opened (a part stamps `opened` once; older ones keep the previous order). The merge takes the ticked rows by key, rebuilds those rows and refuses anything that is no longer contiguous (`stale`), not compressed, not played to the end (a chapter with a fight still owed), or concurrent in the same world. It anchors on the first chapter it holds, else on the first story's key, and lists every story it swallowed in `bonds`, carried over from merged rows. `absorbedBonds` is built before any walk and asked first by every reader, so a swallowed story is never sent again — not as its summary, and not as its raw beats, which is what a story without a summary falls back to — and is not offered for compression again. The merge prompt labels each piece and names each story once with its part; the merged text must name each story (a whole word of the name, particles aside), or it is refused, the screen says which story was left out, and nothing changes.

**1.8.13 is a world named in Korean, and a phone held sideways.** A location's ID was its name lowercased with everything outside `a-z0-9` turned into dashes (`gs` on the server, `oc` on the client), so `달빛 숲` became an empty ID: the server skipped the place and `/background` refused it, and `숲 1` and `동굴 1` both became `1` and shared one picture. Both sides now keep Unicode letters, marks and digits after NFC (marks too, or Hindi `काला` and `किला` would collide), and cap at 60 code points instead of UTF-16 units; an ASCII name keeps exactly the ID it had. A place already painted under its old lossy ID is still found: the locations document is read through `gsPlaces`, which also answers to each stored place's new ID (from its stored name) without rewriting anything, so nothing is painted twice and two names that used to collide stop sharing. `/background` refuses a slug carrying a path separator, `..` or a control character (`bgSlug`). On iPhone, the package's `:host { all: initial }` undid the Engine's `-webkit-text-size-adjust: 100%`, so Safari enlarged the game's text in landscape; the arena sets it back. The arena caps at `100dvh` (with a `100vh` fallback), a fullscreen arena pads by the safe-area insets, a short landscape screen (`max-height: 500px`) drops the frame's padding, border and shadow and tightens the Settings plates, and a fullscreen toggle the browser cannot honour (iPhone Safari has no element fullscreen) is removed rather than left doing nothing. The 16:9 stage and the orientation handling are unchanged.

**1.8.14 is world creation on a phone held sideways, and a field iPhone zoomed into.** Every screen lives in the 16:9 stage, so on a landscape phone (~874×402) the creator was a 533px-wide box with gutters either side, and its pinned brand row, step tabs, error line and nav took about 250px, leaving the field grid (its only scroll region) 30–70px. On a short landscape screen (`max-height: 500px`) the setup sheet now widens the stage to the arena (`.gf-stage:has(.ob-root)`) and makes `.ob-frame` the one scroll region: the brand and tabs scroll away with the fields, the mark is hidden, the textarea is shorter, and the nav sticks to the bottom on the creator’s `--ink` until a text field has focus (the keyboard is up), when it rejoins the page. Changing step scrolls the frame back to the top and an error scrolls it to the end, where the error sits above the nav (`obScroll`); a desktop frame never scrolls, so both are no-ops there. iOS zooms into any focused field under 16px, and the game’s ramp drew them at 7–9px; the Engine’s own 16px floor stops at the package’s `:host { all: initial }`, so on a coarse pointer the arena sets typed fields to 16px. A mouse keeps the ramp, and the Settings readout and in-game changelog now say 1.8.14.

**1.8.15 is a list that stays where you left it.** Ticking a card for the cast, or a theme for outfits, re-renders the whole list (the cast's `.cs-list` in `bi()`'s `w()`, the theme list in `vi()`) through `innerHTML`. Chromium keeps a scroll box's position across that, but WebKit (every iPhone browser, and Safari on a Mac) resets the list to the top, and every scrolling ancestor with it; on a sideways phone, where 1.8.14 made `.ob-frame` the one scroll region, the whole creator page jumped too. Both renders now note the `scrollTop` of the list and each scrolling ancestor before the rebuild and put them back after it. Changing step still lands at the top (`obScroll`), and the Settings readout and in-game changelog now say 1.8.15.

All generation — world text, cast sheets, portraits, banner art — runs through the Engine profile's own configured model and image connections. The package adds no external services and sends nothing anywhere else.

## Payloads

`client.js` and `server.mjs` are single-file esbuild bundles contributed from the package's own source tree, which is maintained outside this repository. The artifact zip, manifest hashes, and catalog entry are re-derived from the committed payload bytes by the package builder, so they cannot drift from what ships.

From 1.8.13 on, `client.js` and `server.mjs` have been edited by hand here. For each, `bundle-patches.json` names the last contributed bundle (its commit, sha256 and size) and records each later edit, in order, as a find-and-replace pair; `tests/gacha-forge-location-ids.regression.mjs` undoes them on the committed bundle to reach those base bytes and redoes them to reach the manifest's hash, and fails if a new hand edit is not recorded. A new upstream drop should carry these edits, and then becomes the new base with an empty patch list.

Rebuild the artifact and catalog entry from the repository root:

```bash
node scripts/build-gacha-forge-package.mjs
node scripts/test-catalog-lanes.mjs
node scripts/validate-package-locales.mjs
node scripts/validate-catalog.mjs
```
