# Formation combat

Formation is an optional Gacha Forge combat mode alongside Classic. It is based on the maintainer's Formation Combat proposal and reuses existing elements, progression, enemies, rewards, battle dialogue and presentation.

## Rules and player flow (1.24.0)

- Choose Classic or Formation when creating a playthrough. Classic remains the default and is unchanged.
- A team seats up to six characters: one to three Front Row fighters and up to three Back Row supports. At the start of each team turn every living support adds one energy of its own element to the team's shared bank. A team with no supports gains one energy every other turn from its fighters. Energy never expires and has no cap.
- Back Row supports do not act and cannot be attacked; they power the team and carry passives. Front Row fighters act. Each turn the player taps a fighter, chooses its Normal skill, Ultimate, Move / Swap or Hold, and taps its target. A summary lists the plan in order with the energy it spends. Any action can be changed or cleared before **Fight**, and Fight resolves the plan in that order. A target that has fallen is replaced by the weakest valid enemy fighter. An action that can no longer be paid, reached or carried out holds, with a note on the card. Auto plans and plays whole turns.
- Normal skills cost 0–4 energy of any element. Ultimates cost 2–8, of which their element share (1..cost) must be the user's element; Light and Dark stand in for it one for one. Every point spent adds its element's bonus: Fire and Dark damage, Wind crit, an Earth shield, a Water heal for the weakest ally, a Light heal for the user.
- Move / Swap moves a fighter to an empty Back Row seat, swaps it with a support, or brings a support into an empty Front Row seat; whoever arrives in front acts next turn. Hold does nothing and keeps the energy. Since 1.24.5, when a Front Row fighter falls, one of its team's supports steps into the empty seat at once: the next enemy in a defeat order first, then for the player's team a support whose element can pass the enemy's element barrier, then a support that can deal damage or prefers the Front Row, then seat order. The support that steps up acts from its team's next turn. A shuffle, swap or retreat that sets off a death never undoes a step-up or seats a fallen unit. Since 1.25.2 a two-unit move, a planned swap or a Swap effect, seats both units before either one's passive fires, so a revive, Swap, Shuffle or Echo it sets off never sees the seat the mover left as free and never seats a fourth unit in a row. A revive returns a unit to the row it fell in when that row has room, otherwise to the other row, or not at all when both are full. A team loses only when it has nobody left; Echo tokens do not count.
- Effects include damage, area damage, drain, execute, damage over time, heal, restore, shield, regeneration, ATK/DEF/crit buffs and debuffs, stun, silence, taunt, immunity, cleanse, revive, energy, swap and shuffle of the enemy board, and Echo summons. Durations count the target's own turns. A unit that just lost a turn to stun or silence resists the next one. From round 12 healing fades and hits grow stronger, so every fight ends.
- Kits follow the character's preferred row. Front fighters hit or protect. Supports have weak actions, strong passives and situational Ultimates that a team saves for. Hybrids may take a perk that rewards switching rows: a free Ultimate after advancing, or a free recast on retreating. Five-stars may take one signature: a solo-frontline bonus, or Echo tokens that fill empty front seats.
- Bosses keep their mechanics and saved combat dialogue and may set an element barrier or a defeat order, shown on the board and in a rule chip. Generated guides are read leniently: a fixable kit or rule is adjusted rather than paid for again.
- Kits written for the rules before 1.24.0 are remade automatically before a Formation world's next battle, with the usual progress in **Story Continuity**; battles wait for the remake. A cancelled or failed remake leaves the old kits in play, and Continuity offers the remake again. After a cancel a new character still gets its kit; a kit that failed is never generated again by a battle start, which asks for **Retry** in Continuity instead. A remake never cancels story work and never waits on an unfinished fight. Classic worlds never generate Formation kits.
- The server replays every Formation fight from its saved snapshot and the recorded plans before granting rewards. Each saved fight records the engine it started on: a fight started before 1.24.0 is scored by that engine, and a fight started on the current engine only accepts current-engine records. Fights saved before 1.24.5 carry no rules revision and are scored by the rules before 1.24.5, where a team whose Front Row fell lost; one in progress across an earlier update within engine 2 (1.24.0 to 1.24.1, say) took the newer of those rules. Since 1.24.5 a saved fight records its rules revision and is scored by it, so a fight started before 1.25.2 keeps the earlier two-unit move. A tab still running an older engine or older Formation rules (1.24.0 included) must reload before it can start a new Formation fight, and spends nothing until then.
- Existing runs can switch modes in Story Continuity after a confirmation, in both directions. Identity, classic kits, level, equipment, inventory and story are preserved; switching back restores the saved Classic kits.

## Voice and presentation

Show the acting character's portrait with existing elemental effects when using an Ultimate. Preserve reduced motion, readable status, mobile controls and saved themes.

Add an opt-in Combat voice lines control under World Sources Audio. Only voiced characters receive a fixed saved set of four lines: death, enemy defeat and two Ultimate variants. Generate through current voice/provider contracts, persist the resulting audio in package storage, and reuse it on later battles and reloads. No automatic regeneration.

Since 1.24.6 the lines play in Classic too: Classic death events name their killer for kill lines, with no rule change. Preparation starts when the battle screen loads. Voiced means an explicit Engine voice, the persona's own voice for the protagonist, or, with the opt-in Voice other characters toggle, a stable pick from the Engine's NPC default voice pool for any other unit. The server derives each speaker's identity (card, persona or unit) and accepts a unit without a card only while that toggle is on. A free notice says when nobody in the team can be voiced.
